<p>Many film #subtitling questions, such as "How should I render x in the subtitles", can often be answered by asking, "Was it intentional"?</p>
<p>Examples:<br>
Q: Should I replicate the speaker's error in the subtitles?<br>
A: If intentional (for character portrayal, humour, plot, etc), then yes.</p>
<p>Q: Do I include the stuttering or hesitation?<br>
A: If intentional (to portray nervousness, hesitation, or a particular mood), then yes, keep it. Of course if it is very clear from the audio, there is no need to.</p>
<p>Q: Should I match the regional accent or dialect?<br>
A: If intentional (to highlight background, social class, or cultural context), try to reflect it meaningfully.</p>
<p>Q: Should I keep repetitions in the dialogue?<br>
A: If intentional (for emotional emphasis, uncertainty, or character style), and if there is time, sure.</p>
<p>Proving once more that high-quality film subtitles can’t be boxed into automation; each one requires individual decisions, with priorities shifting case by case.</p>
