<p>The English-speaking World Discovers #Subtitles Episode 37 (as always, accompanied by #SquidGame stills): <br>
"As audiences embrace subtitles, translated series have become more common, particularly on streaming services. "<br>
#subtitlingheadlines<br>
by @vulture <br>
<a href="https://www.vulture.com/article/localization-translation-international-streaming.html">https://www.vulture.com/article/localization-translation-international-streaming.html</a></p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>#Subtitlers are always grateful to see their work portrayed and explained, especially in such detail, but let's see some inaccuracies here👇</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>"localization starts with a revised script often referred to as a 'template'".<br>
The template is not a script, but a timed text, a subtitle file - edited, positioned, researched, annotated - intended as a basis for all translations. Not necessarily a verbatim transcription.</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>The #subtitlingtemplate is based on the post-production script, if present, ideally including everything the article describes: "notes to explain the context of certain cultural phrases, words, slang terms, honorifics".<br>
So, localization ideally starts from the client script.</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>"Building a #subtitle barrier"<br>
That's a nice play on Bong Joon Ho's words, but this is not what we try to do. Most subtitlers like to think they remove language barriers.</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>And here's some messing up of terms<br>
"The style guides used by Netflix and Disney+ specify that the number of English subtitle characters that can be displayed onscreen at any given moment — known as 'line segmenting' — is 42 per line"</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>'Line segmenting' is breaking the lines at logical points, whether within the same subtitle (up to two lines) or between two subtitles. <br>
What the article refers to is known as "characters per line".</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>There is also the "characters per second" specification, which is probably what the author tries to explain, very unsuccessfully, here:<br>
"the exact number of frames a line of dialogue can be onscreen before moving onto the next line — “timing” — is 20."</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>"Timing" is defining the in and out times of the subtitles. Depending on the specifications, it can be a complicated task, based on many (sometimes contradicting) factors.</p>
{% endraw %}
