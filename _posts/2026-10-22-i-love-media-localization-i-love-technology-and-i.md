<p>I love media localization. I love technology. And I love workflows. So chairing a session called Workflows &amp; Technology at Languages &amp; The Media 2026 sounds like my kind of fun!</p>
<p>Media localization workflows have been a recurring theme in my professional and academic life. I've worked with them, studied them, given talks about them, and written two book chapters about them. And there are always new things to explore.</p>
<p>What I enjoy most is zooming out to look beyond individual tasks, to see how people, tools, and priorities &amp; constraints are organized together. How are things are done? Why? Could they be done differently?</p>
<p>And now that AI brings changes to media localization, workflows are becoming even more interesting, because there's much more happening than automating tasks. Even something that looks as straightforward as introducing AI-generated subtitles raises questions about who does what and who makes the final decisions. Then theres the possibility of translators becoming developers of the tools they use, or voice cloning introducing new questions about consent and accountability.</p>
<p>I'm therefore delighted to be chairing a session featuring:<br>
🔹 Hanne Croonen, on designing human-centered automation.<br>
🔹 Jan-Hendrik Hein and Steve Hale, on transforming localization supply chains in the cloud.<br>
🔹 Mirko Lorenz, on language professionals becoming developers of AI-powered tools.<br>
🔹 Alfonso Carlos Rodríguez Fernández-Peña, on voice cloning, voice forgery, and the legal challenges of AI-generated voices.</p>
<p>These four presentations offer different perspectives, and I'm looking forward to discussing how we choose to organize work around technology, and what those choices mean for those involved.</p>
<p>See you in London on Friday, 6 November!</p>
