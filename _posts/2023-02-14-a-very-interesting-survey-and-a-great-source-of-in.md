I am considering preparing a talk called "Misconceptions about subtitling conventions" if anyone cares to invite me :) <a href="https://twitter.com/JanPedersen1969/status/1625409343381245953">https://twitter.com/JanPedersen1969/status/1625409343381245953</a></p>

{% raw %}
<p>Misconception no 1 about subtitling conventions, norms &amp; guidelines:<br>
❌They are not rules.<br>
✅ They set the priorities.<br>
👉Example: <br>
Longer subtitles with a complete meaning are easier to read (priority = readability). <br>
But meaningful pauses or breaks trump complete meaning.</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>Misconception no 2:<br>
❌Subtitles are not the target text.<br>
✅ The subtitled audiovisual text is the target text.<br>
👉 Viewers do not rely only on subtitles to retrieve meaning. The image and the sound provide more information than you'd think <br>
(Aka the "Viewers are not idiots" rule)</p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>Misconception no 3:<br>
❌  You can't judge subtitles (content or form) by looking at them in isolation.<br>
✅ Subtitles have a cumulative effect.<br>
👉 You need to watch the whole product to decide whether a strategy works, and understand its side-effects.</p>
{% endraw %}
