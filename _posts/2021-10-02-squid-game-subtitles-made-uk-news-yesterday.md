<p>"Go away" may be a perfect translation for "What are you looking at". But #subtitling is a vulnerable translation mode, so subtitles often get the that's-not-what-he-said treatment. Especially when translation is perceived as word-for-word equivalence<br>

{% raw %}
<p>Subtitlers are aware that viewers always compare with audio and they have their strategies. But in this case, the CC subtitles made it possible to compare the original audio with the dubbed version. Thus making #dubbing a vulnerable translation mode as well, when it rarely is.</p>

<p>#Translation seems to be commonly thought of as a literal word-for-word written rendering of the sacred single-meaning original. And that there is a Platonic ideal single true translation: "the whole truth and nothing but the truth". All else is fail<br>
<a href="https://www-cbc-ca.cdn.ampproject.org/c/s/www.cbc.ca/amp/1.6202592">https://www-cbc-ca.cdn.ampproject.org/c/s/www.cbc.ca/amp/1.6202592</a></p>
{% endraw %}

{% raw %}
<p>Hence "the impossibilities of translation" and the birth of terms like localization and transcreation, whose definition starts with "this is not translation"</p>
