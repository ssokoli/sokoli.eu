<p>Now that AI can generate “products” faster than any human, I’ve become increasingly interested in process-oriented versus product-oriented teaching - especially tricky in distance education.</p>
<p>Traditionally, process-based pedagogy meant valuing the journey of learning over the final result. In practice, students are often asked to produce evidence of process: reflections, journals, drafts - and now annotations in their conversations with AI. In trying to document learning, we risk reproducing the very product orientation we claim to move away from.</p>
<p>Any assignment I can think of, AI can now generate as a polished “output” on demand.</p>
<p>So how do we get out of this loop?<br>
My only idea for now is to:<br>
 - create authentic communicative spaces where learning happens through interaction rather than submission<br>
 - value traces of engagement that emerge naturally, rather than artifacts produced just to satisfy assessment.</p>
<p>I think there is an app for that, but it's costly. So I'll need to make do with the resources I have.</p>
<p>Big thanks to Jason Gulya for inspiring this. If you don't follow him, you should.</p>
