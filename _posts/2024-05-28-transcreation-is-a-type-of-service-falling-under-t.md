<p>#Transcreation is a type of service falling under the great umbrella of #translation and #languageservices.</p>
<p>It's not a strategy or technique, and doesn't mean translating "freely" or "creatively" or "not literally". Translation is an inherently creative process. And creativity permeates all translation types and services.</p>
<p>If in doubt when using the term, add "services" after every mention of transcreation, even mentally. If "transcreation services" doesn't fit in the discussion, try another term for the translation technique (micro-level, such as adaptation, explicitation, semantic, communicative, idiomatic etc) or strategy (broader approach, such as target-oriented, source-oriented etc) you want to refer to. Newmark, Vinay &amp; Dalbernet and other scholars have useful categorizations.</p>
<p>Btw, if you check Reiss &amp; Vermeer's Skopos theory (despite the not very sexy Greek name) their definition of translation is exactly what many people today call transcreation.</p>
