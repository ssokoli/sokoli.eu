<p>I recently read a piece selling an AI-powered solution for #medialocalization as “a complete, structured #understanding of the source content” that can generate #subtitles and dubs “as if a human did it, but consistently, at scale, and at a fraction of the time and cost.”</p>
<p>To explain what great localization looks like, the author used an image of his own memory: a translator surrounded by posters and toys from Game of Thrones, literally immersing herself in context.</p>
<p>That is the image viewers should have of good localization: a human being entering the world of the story before carrying it into another language. Someone who cares about the content creator and the viewers, and is accountable for how the work is received.</p>
<p>I wonder about the irony of using that image of human immersion to sell “contextual understanding” as machine infrastructure. If understanding is the value, should we be so quick to remove the human who understands?</p>
