<p>After the initial excitement, time to delve into a factual error in the video script.</p>
<p>Namely the statement that "The modern approach to #subtitling in most languages is that reading is faster than talking, and that even for fast talking, you put every word on screen. The viewer can always pause."</p>
<p>For starters, not all viewers can read as quickly as they can listen.</p>
<p>And pausing to read the subtitles?</p>
<p>This is not a standard approach in professional subtitling, particularly for media content intended for uninterrupted viewing, such as films or television broadcasts.</p>
<p>Most importantly, for visually-driven content - unlike "talking heads" videos in YouTube - the image is crucial for the viewer's full comprehension and enjoyment. Viewers need to watch not only read.</p>
<p>Which means that we must balance the need to</p>
<p>👉 convey the meaning of the dialogue</p>
<p>👉 make the subtitles readable at a comfortable pace for the viewers</p>
<p>👉 allowing them to enjoy the image.</p>
<p>Thank you Agnieszka Szarkowska , Diana Renker and Lindsay Bywood for pointing that out!</p>
