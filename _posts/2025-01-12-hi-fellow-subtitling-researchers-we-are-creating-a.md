<p>Hi, fellow #subtitling researchers - we are creating a list of areas of research in subtitling. What do you think of this one? Something to add or remove? They are not in any particular order, but I am numbering them for ease of reference:</p>
<p>1. Subtitling workflows and technology (templates, transcription, timing, QA in large-scale operations, impact of AI tools)<br>
2. Subtitling for accessibility: SDH, CC (creativity vs standardization)<br>
3. Intralingual subtitles and non-standard uses (CC without tags to be used with the original audio, dubtitles, aesthetically integrated aka "creative" subtitles)<br>
4. Authors' rights and ethical considerations (reuse of subtitlers' work, e.g. to create a dubbing script, ethical implications of AI-assisted workflows)<br>
5. Creativity and imagination in subtitling (strategies and techniques to address units of creative potential: e.g. puns, humour, third languages)<br>
6. Viewer reception studies (comprehension and cognitive load, subtitle presentation speed versus viewers' reading speed, impact of non-standard subtitles)<br>
7. Working conditions, client expectations, new roles<br>
8. Pivot subtitling - Low-resource languages<br>
9. Subtitling pedagogy and training<br>
10. Translation Studies and Subtitling Theory (how subtitling contributes to the broader field of translation studies)</p>
