<p>About cost savings: are they good news for everyone involved?</p>
<p>In this interview, Ben Affleck describes InterPositive, the AI company he founded, which Netflix bought in March 2026 for $587 million. He describes shooting about eight months of his own footage to fine-tune open video models, which were trained on video from the internet, so that they meet "cinematic" standards without training on other filmmakers' work. Each production then trains its own model on its own footage, and the tool is used mainly in post-production (backgrounds, changes to shots). He presents this as ethical and as adding to human work, not replacing it.</p>
<p>The company's patent application, as reported by Deadline*, says its tools will save money. Affleck says that films using the tools employ the same number of people as before. So where do the savings come from? The patent application expects them to come from people's work: from extras, from visual effects artists, and from days of filming, because the crew is paid for each day they work. Extras are not counted as crew, so they can lose work while the crew stays the same.</p>
<p>So, if the same number of people are to make a living from their work, one of the following must happen:<br>
a) they work on more content, to get the same number of days' work<br>
b) they get a rate increase, (which would reduce the savings)<br>
c) new kind of work is required. More paid days could be dedicated to data preparation or operating the AI tools. But we don't know whether the same people do that work, or whether it is paid at the same rate.</p>
<p>If none of these makes up the difference, some people leave the profession. In that case, "the same number of people" is not true across the industry, even if it is true on each production.</p>
<p>So, $587 million changed hands, in exchange for future savings. Who receives those savings?</p>
